# 4.7 — Anomaly Interpretation Workflow

Scientific interpretation is a workflow, not a single decision. Every anomaly should pass through a systematic process before reaching a biological conclusion.

## Part 1 — The Complete Workflow

Observed NDVI → Construct Baseline → Detect Anomaly → Describe Anomaly (Direction, Magnitude, Duration, Timing) → Locate Change Point → Generate Multiple Hypotheses → Collect Supporting Evidence → Reject Unsupported Explanations → Most Plausible Interpretation

### Step 1: Observe the Signal

Start with the NDVI time series. Do not interpret anything yet.

Simply ask

What does the curve look like?

**Examples**
- smooth
- noisy
- missing observations
- unexpected drops
- unexpected increases

### Step 2: Construct the Baseline

Compare the observations with an expected seasonal pattern.

**Ask**
- Which baseline am I using?
- Why this baseline?
- What assumptions does it make?

### Step 3: Detect the Anomalies

Now compare

Observation − Baseline = Anomaly

**Identify**
- positive anomalies
- negative anomalies
- near-zero anomalies

Still, no interpretation.

### Step 4: Describe Every Anomaly

- **Direction** → Positive or negative
- **Magnitude** → Small, Moderate or Large
- **Duration** → Short, Medium or Long
- **Timing**

During
- emergence
- tillering
- heading
- grain filling
- senescence

### Step 5: Locate Change Points

Where did the behavior begin changing?

**Determine**
- abrupt change
- gradual change
- temporary disturbance
- permanent shift

### Step 6: Generate Multiple Hypotheses

**Biological**
- drought
- flood
- disease
- pests

**Management**
- harvest
- irrigation
- fertilizer
- planting delay

**Observation**
- cloud
- shadow
- interpolation
- sensor artifact

Multiple explanations should always remain possible.

### Step 7: Evaluate Evidence

Now examine supporting information.

**Examples**
- rainfall records
- flood reports
- cloud masks
- field observations
- crop calendar
- neighboring fields

### Step 8: Reject Unsupported Explanations

Scientific reasoning works by elimination.

**Example**

Observation → Negative anomaly.

Possible explanations
- drought
- harvest
- cloud

**Evidence**

Harvest has not occurred.

No clouds.

Rainfall is extremely low.

**Remaining explanation**

Drought becomes the strongest hypothesis.

**Notice**

You rejected alternatives before accepting one.

### Step 9 — Final Interpretation

Your final interpretation should be careful.

Instead of

*"Drought caused the anomaly."*

Write

*A persistent negative anomaly occurred during heading. Based on prolonged rainfall deficit and the absence of cloud contamination or harvest activity, the anomaly is most consistent with drought stress.*

That wording reflects scientific reasoning.
